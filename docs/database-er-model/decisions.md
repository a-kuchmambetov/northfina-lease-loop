# Data and lifecycle decisions

- Import `assets.json` and `customers.json` using their stable IDs. Customer `profile_json` holds type-specific fields such as birth date, business ID, income and beneficial owners. Validate the shape by customer type; protect this sensitive data appropriately. Keep existing asset make, model, model year and timestamps even though the diagram abbreviates them.
- An application concerns one physical asset and one customer. Persist only currently applicable answers when answers change; retain the decision and its reasons along with a question-bank version or content hash. The answer value is JSON because question types differ. Link a quote to the same asset and customer as its application.
- A quote records the price list and coefficient versions **and the values used**. The signing policy must explicitly decide whether an old quote is still valid; once signed, copy the agreed values into the contract snapshot. A contract's net price must not change when reference files change. `pricing_snapshot_json` includes list price, down payment, financed amount, factor, buyout and source versions.
- Each physical item can have many sequential lives, but only one active lease at a time. One contract covers one asset in this model; a fleet application needs multiple contracts or a future contract-line model. `(asset_id, sequence_number)` is unique. `previous_lives` in imported assets is a baseline: historic lives missing from the pack must not be invented or counted twice.
- An invoice snapshots the VAT rate applicable on its **invoice date**, not the signing date, and its net, VAT and total in integer cents. `(contract_id, sequence_number)` is unique. Store the rate as an exact decimal string or scaled integer, never a binary float. Define rounding and calendar rules in domain logic before issuing invoices.
- A life closes through buyout, return, pass-on or early exit. Record the calculation and the condition and usage observed at closure. Refurbishment records costs and counter resets before the next life; immutable events and audits form the item passport. A waiting request is optional because new leases need not come from the waiting list. Its source `who` is free text, not necessarily a known customer. Record a real submission timestamp or another explicit stable tie-breaker when importing demand; do not assume its ID is arrival order.
- Prices, VAT rates, coefficient tables, questions, residual curves, refurbishment costs and CO₂ factors come from their corresponding `data/*.json` files, validated by `src/data/schemas.ts`. Version or hash reference inputs used for decisions and persist calculation inputs/results where historical reproducibility matters. CO₂ is derived from life and refurbishment history plus a recorded factor version; it is not money.
- ISO calendar dates are `YYYY-MM-DD`; timestamps are for actual events. Dates in reference data have inclusive ends. All monetary amounts are integer euro cents. Check nonnegative amounts, valid status transitions, asset availability, non-overlapping lives and consistency of linked asset/customer in the transaction layer. Use `withAudit()` and its transaction for every change, including the audit record. The database must enforce foreign keys and the unique keys described above where possible.
